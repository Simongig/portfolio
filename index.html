Topics

Currently there are no detailed texts available. But you can get a grasp of the topics below.

  • LLM Poisoning - This is actually more referenced as a type of attack on LLMs, but it seems to me that this is more turning into a necessary technique to preserve ownership of digital content. Since AI companies scrape the whole internet everything will be fed into the LLMs training. I would not want this, so from my perspective it is not an attack, rather than a necessary safety mechanism to protect digital content.
  • RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) - Beside the neverending hype of LLMs, this is a technology I find a genuinely helpful. LLMs by design can hallucinate and even if they are telling the truth you never know where information is coming from. RAG uses a database of curated information. The retrieval gets the sources and the LLM generates an answer or can act as an agent to improve output. You always have the source from what the answer was generated, allowing you to read more from the source or fact check. This makes it well suited if you don't want your answer to be slop, but actually high quality information (e.g. for healthcare or other areas).
  • Health Tech - In the last point I mentioned healthcare. This is another very interesting topic since AI gains more ground in this area. But AI is often non deterministic. In contrast physicians prefer tangible information to build their diagnosis on (which is a very good thing). But it is easier to market your AI tool if it shows an impressive AUC on "sick or not sick". But no physician should ever rely on this to recommend a treatment or surgery. It is often very hard or impossible to understand what the algorithm actually "learned". AI in the healthcare field should assist physicians to find biomarkers or other evidence which can be discussed rather than taking decisions itself.
  • Digital Sovereignty - There are a ton of good european, open-source products that are more than capable of replacing well marketed trans atlantic software. And a lot of them are free and/or don't use your data as the product. What I like to use: Ubuntu (Linux itself can feel like macOS, if you have a bit of technical knowledge, except for air drop.. that's very convient I have to admit. But there are also open source alternatives to that). Proton Mail offers a suite like Google but is more privacy focused and has cool features like alias emails that route e-mails to your inbox but you can delete them if you get trash mail (Like contact-me.lbvgw@aleeas.com where you can contact me). Libre Office and GIMP I worked with MS Office and Photoshop (also professionally) - It is completely fine for most of the tasks. Don't let marketing or convenienc fool you.
  • Nutritioning - Once I watched a documentary on Physarum polycephalum or "Blob", which apparently you can also grow at home... if you want that. It is a fungus that "knows" how to nutrition itself optimally if given multiple sources of nutrients. I found this fascinating and made me wonder how this can be achieved for humans. Nutritioning is a complex topic and gets even more complex if you perform sports extensively or competetively. But it is one of the most fundamently building blocks of a healthy life. I am currently trying to build a recipes app to facilitate and support with dietary needs.
